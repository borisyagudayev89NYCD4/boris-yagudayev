SOURCES & CONTEXT

Sources

Official records, research and independent reporting relevant to statements and policy proposals described on this site. A source is included because it provides factual context, evidence, or a real-world analogue directly relevant to a stated position—not because the source necessarily endorses that position.

How to read this page

Policy questions often involve tradeoffs and uncertainty. Sources below are selected to document the factual background of a proposal, describe comparable policy structures, or identify evidence relevant to likely effects. Where a source identifies competing effects, those findings are noted rather than presented as one-sided support. For proposals that are novel or not yet implemented in the form Yagudayev describes, sources are labeled as precedents or analogues rather than evidence that the proposal itself has been proven.

ECONOMIC POLICY & WAGES

Wages, income and employment

Congressional Budget Office — Federal minimum wage

CBO analyzes how alternative federal minimum-wage increases can affect earnings, employment, family income and poverty. Its research finds higher earnings for many low-wage workers while also estimating potential employment reductions, with substantial uncertainty about the magnitude of those effects.

CBO: How Increasing the Federal Minimum Wage Could Affect Employment and Family Income

Congressional Budget Office — Raise the Wage Act

CBO's analysis of the 2021 proposal provides a concrete example of the distributional and budgetary tradeoffs involved in setting a substantially higher federal wage floor.

CBO: The Budgetary Effects of the Raise the Wage Act of 2021

HEALTHCARE

Single-payer system design

Congressional Budget Office — Single-payer design

CBO identifies core features and design choices of single-payer systems, including universal or broad eligibility, required financing, government payment of covered services and the possible supplemental role of private insurance. These issues directly relate to Yagudayev's proposed universal public system with optional supplemental private services.

CBO: Key Design Components and Considerations for Establishing a Single-Payer Health Care System

Congressional Budget Office — Modeling single payer

CBO has also modeled illustrative Medicare-based single-payer systems and explains how provider payment rates, cost sharing, covered benefits and long-term services can materially change federal costs and other outcomes.

CBO: How CBO Analyzes Proposals for a Single-Payer Health Care System

HOUSING

Housing supply & affordability

HUD — Housing supply and land-use policy

HUD's policy research describes limited housing supply and rapid price appreciation as important concerns in many metropolitan areas and examines how state and local land-use policy can influence housing production. The research also emphasizes that housing-market conditions differ across places and policy should account for those differences.

HUD User: How Can State Governments Influence Local Zoning to Support Healthier Housing Markets?

HUD — Evidence and uncertainty around new supply

HUD's Cityscape research reviews competing findings about the relationship between new construction and affordability, including evidence that effects can differ by market segment. It provides context for Yagudayev's proposal to combine increased supply with affordability measures rather than treating construction alone as the entire housing policy.

HUD User: Cityscape, March 2024

LABOR & THE FUTURE OF WORK

Collective bargaining, worker voice, employment & ownership

OECD — Collective bargaining and workers' voice

The OECD's cross-country review examines collective bargaining in relation to wages, wage dispersion, employment, job quality and technological change. It finds that bargaining institutions can reduce some forms of wage inequality while emphasizing that outcomes depend on how bargaining systems are designed.

OECD: Negotiating Our Way Up

International Labour Organization — Reduced working hours

The ILO has long recognized progressive reductions in normal working hours as a labor-policy objective, including the forty-hour-week standard. This provides historical policy context for proposals to reduce the standard workweek, although it does not by itself establish the effects of a four-day schedule in the United States.

ILO: Reduction of Hours of Work Recommendation

Levy Economics Institute — Job guarantee design

Research from the Levy Economics Institute develops a detailed job-guarantee model built around federally funded, locally administered voluntary employment available on demand. This is a close policy analogue to Yagudayev's stated full-employment principle that a person who wants to work should have access to a job. It is a policy proposal and research program, not evidence that a nationwide U.S. job guarantee has already been implemented.

Levy Economics Institute: The Job Guarantee — Design, Jobs, and Implementation

NBER — Employee ownership and profit sharing

NBER research using worker surveys examines employee ownership, profit sharing and broad-based stock options. It reports associations with outcomes including worker effort, loyalty and turnover, particularly when shared compensation is combined with employee involvement, training and job security. This provides evidence relevant to Yagudayev's interest in worker stakes in company stock and profits while not establishing that every ownership structure produces the same effects.

NBER: Creating a Bigger Pie? The Effects of Employee Ownership, Profit Sharing, and Stock Options on Workplace Performance

NBER — Employee ownership and worker wealth

A related NBER study examines whether shared-capitalism arrangements substitute for conventional compensation and how they relate to worker wealth. The authors report no evidence in their datasets that company stock ownership displaced pay or benefits and find that company ownership increased employee wealth, while noting limited effects on the overall distribution of wealth.

NBER: Show Me the Money — Does Shared Capitalism Share the Wealth?

ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE

Economic change, public wealth, risk management & international standards

IMF staff research — AI and work

An IMF Staff Discussion Note examines AI exposure across labor markets and the potential for both productivity gains and job displacement, as well as implications for income and wealth inequality. The paper provides economic context for Yagudayev's proposals concerning AI-era labor policy and broader participation in AI-generated wealth.

IMF: Gen-AI — Artificial Intelligence and the Future of Work

U.S. Congress — AI sovereign wealth fund proposal

In June 2026, S. 4825, the American A.I. Sovereign Wealth Fund Act, was introduced in the U.S. Senate and referred to the Finance Committee. The introduced bill proposes an excise tax on systemically important AI activity and a sovereign-wealth-fund structure. It is not law, and its tax design is not identical to Yagudayev's proposed AI value-added tax, but it is a direct federal legislative analogue for capturing part of AI-related economic activity for a public fund.

U.S. Government Publishing Office: S. 4825 — American A.I. Sovereign Wealth Fund Act

NIST — AI Risk Management Framework

NIST developed the AI Risk Management Framework with public- and private-sector participation to help organizations manage risks associated with AI. Its work provides an existing example of government-industry collaboration on AI governance.

NIST: AI Risk Management Framework

NIST — International AI standards

NIST identifies alignment with international standards as a priority of its AI risk-management work and coordinates with government and industry stakeholders on international standards activities. This is directly relevant to Yagudayev's proposal for international AI governance and verification structures.

NIST: Artificial Intelligence Standards

U.S. Government Accountability Office — AI accountability

GAO's AI accountability framework addresses governance, data, performance and monitoring and discusses auditing and third-party assessment. It provides a governmental reference point for questions of AI oversight and verification.

GAO: Artificial Intelligence Accountability Framework

DEMOCRACY & CAMPAIGN FINANCE

Campaign finance & digital participation

Federal Election Commission — Citizens United

The FEC's case summary explains that the Supreme Court invalidated restrictions on corporate independent expenditures and electioneering communications while upholding disclosure and disclaimer requirements and leaving the prohibition on direct corporate contributions intact. This provides the legal context for Yagudayev's stated desire to overturn the constitutional rule established by the decision.

FEC: Citizens United v. FEC

Federal Election Commission — Public financing

The FEC documents the existing federal public-funding system for presidential campaigns. Yagudayev's proposal would apply public financing and matching more broadly to federal campaigns and therefore goes beyond this existing framework.

FEC: Public Funding of Presidential Elections

United Kingdom — Government and Parliament e-petitions

The United Kingdom operates an official digital petition system through which eligible citizens and residents can create or sign petitions seeking changes to law or government policy. Signature thresholds can trigger a government response or consideration for parliamentary debate. It provides a real-world analogue for the Citizens App concept of digitally authenticated public participation, but it does not include the full candidate-registration, legislative polling or social-media functions Yagudayev proposes.

GOV.UK: Petition Parliament and the Government

European Union — Verified online support for citizens' initiatives

The European Citizens' Initiative uses an official online collection system in which supporters provide personal data required by their country for verification, with privacy protections governing that data. This demonstrates that government-administered digital collection and verification of citizen support can be implemented at large scale, while the Citizens App would require its own U.S. legal, privacy and cybersecurity framework.

European Commission: European Citizens' Initiative Online Collection System

European Union — Digital identity and electronic signatures

The EU Digital Identity framework provides another relevant technical precedent: verified identity credentials can be used for online public services, while selective disclosure is intended to limit the personal information a user must reveal. Qualified electronic signatures can have the legal effect of handwritten signatures. These technologies illustrate possible building blocks for secure civic authentication, although they do not establish how U.S. election signatures or ballot access should be regulated.

European Commission: Digital Identity Wallet — PID Identification

FOREIGN POLICY

International-law, territorial & diplomatic context

United Nations — Israel and Palestine

The UN Secretary-General stated in February 2026 that the UN's two-state framework envisions Israel and an independent Palestinian state living side by side in peace and security on the basis of the pre-1967 lines. This provides international-policy context for Yagudayev's stated territorial framework; it does not imply UN endorsement of the specific intervention mechanism he proposes.

United Nations: Secretary-General remarks, February 3, 2026

United Nations — Security arrangements in a two-state framework

The 2025 New York Declaration discusses security guarantees for both Israelis and Palestinians, international support for Palestinian security institutions, and a two-state solution based on the 1967 lines. These elements provide context for proposals involving an international role during a political transition.

United Nations: New York Declaration on the Two-State Solution

United Nations — Status of Russia-occupied Ukrainian territory

UN General Assembly documentation continues to describe Crimea and other Russian-occupied areas as temporarily occupied territories of Ukraine. This is important context for Yagudayev's proposed negotiated settlement under which Russia would retain territory it currently controls: that proposal would represent a departure from the internationally recognized territorial status documented by the United Nations.

United Nations Digital Library: A/RES/80/223

United Nations human-rights system — Democratic rights in Russia

UN human-rights documentation has called on Russian authorities to protect political participation, freedom of expression, assembly and association and a democratic election process. This provides factual context for Yagudayev's proposal to condition a negotiated territorial settlement on verifiable democratic reforms inside Russia; it does not endorse that proposed exchange.

United Nations human-rights documentation on the Russian Federation

U.S. State Department — U.S. relationship with Taiwan

Current State Department guidance states that the United States recognizes the People's Republic of China as the sole legal government of China, does not recognize Taiwan as an independent sovereign state, and maintains economic, cultural and other relations with Taiwan under the U.S. one-China policy and the Taiwan Relations Act. This establishes the existing U.S. policy background against which Yagudayev's proposed China-Taiwan diplomatic framework would operate.

U.S. Department of State: Foreign Affairs Manual — Taiwan relations

Political rights in China — independent assessment

Freedom House's 2026 country report describes China's national political system as lacking direct or competitive elections for national executive leadership and meaningful organized political opposition. This provides independent context for Yagudayev's call for democratic opening in China. Freedom House is an independent nongovernmental organization, not a neutral governmental authority, and its conclusions should be read as its documented assessment.

Freedom House: China — Freedom in the World 2026
