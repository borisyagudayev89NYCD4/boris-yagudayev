STATED POLICY POSITIONS

Positions

A developing reference to policy positions described by Boris Yagudayev. The page distinguishes stated goals from policy mechanisms that remain under development and does not independently endorse the positions presented.

Yagudayev describes his economic outlook as historically informed and focused on reducing economic inequality, strengthening the position of workers and ensuring access to basic needs while retaining room for private enterprise and innovation.

Specific tax rates, benefit levels and several implementation mechanisms have not yet been specified.

ECONOMIC POLICY

Rebalancing, wages & public investment

Yagudayev supports increasing taxation on high incomes, large corporations and capital gains as part of a broader effort to reduce wealth and income inequality. He also supports reducing tax burdens on small businesses where feasible.

He supports a federal living-wage framework that would account for differences in the cost of a required basket of goods across different areas rather than relying exclusively on a single national cost assumption.

His broader approach favors public spending directed toward workers, families, healthcare and technological priorities. He describes shelter, healthcare and access to work as economic rights rather than solely market commodities.

Yagudayev also supports strategically reducing military spending. The scale, timetable and programs affected by such reductions have not yet been specified.

HEALTHCARE

Universal single-payer coverage

Yagudayev supports a single-payer Medicare for All system providing universal healthcare coverage. Under his proposed framework, everyone would participate in and contribute to the universal system at levels designed to sustain universal coverage.

He proposes comprehensive coverage of medically necessary care, including primary and specialist care, hospitalization, prescription drugs, mental and behavioral healthcare, dental, vision, hearing, reproductive care and long-term care.

His proposal would permit a supplemental private market for elective, boutique or additional services. Purchasing private services would not exempt an individual from contributing to the universal public system.

The precise financing structure and contribution rates remain to be developed.

HOUSING

More homes & new community models

Yagudayev supports policies intended to substantially increase the American housing stock and an active governmental role in facilitating new construction. He also supports stronger measures aimed at controlling rental costs while increasing housing supply.

He favors renewed suburban development and exploring community designs that incorporate changes in how Americans work, including remote and hybrid employment.

He is exploring public-private development models connecting housing construction, employment stability, workforce development and assistance toward homeownership. In this concept, workers could have a greater stake in the communities they help construct.

The specific design of rent regulation, homeownership assistance and work-linked housing programs remains under development.

LABOR & THE FUTURE OF WORK

Worker power in a changing economy

Yagudayev supports greater unionization and stronger worker participation as artificial intelligence, automation and other technological changes alter employment and productivity.

His stated policy direction includes movement toward a four-day workweek, a universal-income system and a federal full-employment commitment. He describes full employment as a principle that people who want to work should have access to employment rather than prolonged involuntary unemployment.

He also supports increased employer-matched retirement contributions, worker representation on corporate boards and mechanisms that provide workers with greater participation in company stock ownership and profits.

Benefit amounts, work-hour requirements, the structure of a full-employment program and ownership formulas have not yet been specified.

ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE

Public participation in AI-era wealth

Yagudayev supports a proactive governmental role in the development and regulation of artificial intelligence. He favors exploring public-private ownership and investment structures that could give the public a financial stake in technologies capable of substantially changing employment and productivity.

Over the medium and long term, he supports creating a national wealth fund connected to economic gains generated by AI. One mechanism he proposes exploring is a value-added tax on AI-related products and services, with public revenues directed toward such a fund.

Internationally, Yagudayev supports pursuing a rules-based framework for artificial intelligence that would establish processes for regulation, verification and international cooperation, including rules addressing military uses of AI, AI-enabled crime and economic disruption.

The tax base and rate, fund governance, public-private ownership model and international verification structure remain proposals for further development.

DEMOCRACY & CAMPAIGN FINANCE

Participation, access & public financing

Yagudayev supports public financing and public matching for federal campaigns, ranked-choice voting and reforms intended to expand access to the electoral process for voters and prospective candidates.

He supports overturning the constitutional rule established by Citizens United v. FEC, including through constitutionally permissible reforms. The Supreme Court's 2010 decision invalidated federal prohibitions on corporate independent expenditures and electioneering communications while leaving disclosure requirements and the ban on direct corporate contributions in place.

Existing federal public campaign financing is centered on presidential elections. Yagudayev's position would extend a public-financing or matching framework to federal congressional campaigns and therefore would require new federal legislation.

His broader stated principle is greater direct voter participation and more equal access to political candidacy.

Legal context: See the Federal Election Commission's materials on Citizens United v. FEC and public funding of presidential elections.

PROPOSAL IN DEVELOPMENT

The “Citizens App”

Yagudayev proposes exploring a publicly funded civic social-media platform, which he calls the Citizens App. The proposed platform would organize civic information according to a verified user's jurisdiction, allowing people to identify government offices and officeholders from the local through federal levels.

Under the concept, users could choose to examine pending and proposed legislation and participate in nonbinding public polling on those measures. Yagudayev envisions the resulting polling as an additional source of constituent feedback for elected officials.

The proposal also contemplates candidate profiles, candidate registration functions and electronic support for ballot-access petitions where applicable law permits them. Yagudayev proposes that jurisdictions be able to opt into the system rather than having the platform automatically replace existing state and local election procedures.

Important implementation questions—including identity verification, privacy, cybersecurity, separation of verified identity from political-expression data, election-law compliance and administration—remain to be developed.

OTHER DOCUMENTED POSITIONS

Cannabis policy

Yagudayev has publicly supported cannabis legalization and liberalization of federal cannabis policy.

Drug policy & social assistance

He has advocated addressing drug-related social problems with greater emphasis on assistance, inclusion and underlying social conditions rather than primarily through enforcement.

Political identity

Yagudayev has publicly used Democratic and communist terminology to describe his political identity and has also associated his politics with democratic socialism.

Political communication

He has described social media as a modern public square and uses online platforms as a principal venue for his political communication.
