STATED POLICY POSITIONS

Positions

A developing reference to policy positions described by Boris Yagudayev. The page distinguishes stated goals from policy mechanisms that remain under development and does not independently endorse the positions presented.

Boris describes his economic outlook as historically informed and focused on reducing economic inequality, strengthening the position of workers and ensuring access to basic needs while retaining room for private enterprise and innovation.

Specific tax rates, benefit levels and several implementation mechanisms have not yet been specified.

▥

ECONOMIC POLICY

Rebalancing, wages & public investment

“After decades of policies that have pushed wealth and resources toward the 1 percent and away from everyday Americans, it’s about time we begin reversing that trend if the United States is to live up to its ideals.”

Boris supports increasing taxation on high incomes, large corporations and capital gains as part of a broader effort to reduce wealth and income inequality. He also supports reducing tax burdens on small businesses where feasible.

He supports a federal living-wage framework that would account for differences in the cost of a required basket of goods across different areas rather than relying exclusively on a single national cost assumption.

His broader approach favors public spending directed toward workers, families, healthcare and technological priorities. He describes shelter, healthcare and access to work as economic rights rather than solely market commodities.

Boris also supports strategically reducing military spending. The scale, timetable and programs affected by such reductions have not yet been specified.

HEALTHCARE

Universal single-payer coverage

“The right to live a healthy life without going into debt is a universal and natural right for all.”

Boris supports a single-payer Medicare for All system providing universal healthcare coverage. Under his proposed framework, everyone would participate in and contribute to the universal system at levels designed to sustain universal coverage.

He proposes comprehensive coverage of medically necessary care, including primary and specialist care, hospitalization, prescription drugs, mental and behavioral healthcare, dental, vision, hearing, reproductive care and long-term care.

His proposal would permit a supplemental private market for elective, boutique or additional services. Purchasing private services would not exempt an individual from contributing to the universal public system.

The precise financing structure and contribution rates remain to be developed.

HOUSING

More homes & new community models

“When we build homes, we build bright futures.”

Boris supports policies intended to substantially increase the American housing stock and an active governmental role in facilitating new construction. He also supports stronger measures aimed at controlling rental costs while increasing housing supply.

He favors renewed suburban development and exploring community designs that incorporate changes in how Americans work, including remote and hybrid employment.

He is exploring public-private development models connecting housing construction, employment stability, workforce development and assistance toward homeownership. In this concept, workers could have a greater stake in the communities they help construct.

The specific design of rent regulation, homeownership assistance and work-linked housing programs remains under development.

LABOR & THE FUTURE OF WORK

Worker power in a changing economy

“Dignity and respect for workers will create a stronger, better economy for all.”

Boris supports greater unionization and stronger worker participation as artificial intelligence, automation and other technological changes alter employment and productivity.

His stated policy direction includes movement toward a four-day workweek, a universal-income system and a federal full-employment commitment. He describes full employment as a principle that people who want to work should have access to employment rather than prolonged involuntary unemployment.

He also supports increased employer-matched retirement contributions, worker representation on corporate boards and mechanisms that provide workers with greater participation in company stock ownership and profits.

Benefit amounts, work-hour requirements, the structure of a full-employment program and ownership formulas have not yet been specified.

SOCIAL SECURITY & RETIREMENT

A universal retirement-income floor

“Truly honoring retirees and seniors requires ingenuity in fixing Social Security.”

Boris proposes redesigning Social Security retirement benefits around a universal minimum standard of living in old age. Under his concept, qualifying retirees would receive the same base Social Security benefit rather than a benefit that rises with lifetime earnings. The benefit would be tied to the cost of a defined basket of basic necessities, particularly shelter and food, extending his broader basket-of-goods approach to living wages into retirement policy.

He supports expanding benefits where necessary to meet that minimum and lowering the retirement age rather than increasing it. He also supports allowing retirement eligibility to vary by occupation, with the possibility of earlier eligibility for workers in physically demanding or otherwise burdensome occupations.

For financing, Yagudayev supports considering a combination of higher payroll-tax contributions and removal of the taxable-earnings cap. He also proposes examining whether a larger Social Security floor could replace or reduce some overlapping senior housing assistance, while leaving the precise interaction with existing assistance programs to further policy development.

Under his proposal, retirees with substantial private retirement savings would retain the right to the universal benefit but could voluntarily surrender future Social Security retirement payments in exchange for a defined tax advantage associated with retirement savings or withdrawals. Boris's stated objective is to structure the incentive so that some retirees may prefer a more immediate and certain tax benefit while the Social Security system avoids a larger stream of future payments.

The benefit amount, geographic basket-of-goods formula, retirement ages by occupation, payroll-tax rate, treatment of the taxable maximum, interaction with housing assistance and the actuarial value and tax mechanics of the voluntary opt-out remain to be developed and modeled.

Current-law context: Social Security retirement benefits currently depend in part on a worker's earnings history and claiming age. The Social Security Administration also publishes actuarial estimates for reform options involving changes to payroll-tax rates and the taxable maximum.

EDUCATION & CHILD CARE

Universal access from early childhood through public college

“Supporting students and families is an investment in the future of our rapidly changing world.”

Boris supports universal access to child care, day care and pre-kindergarten and describes public education as a service that should be available without tuition barriers. He supports state-level efforts to establish these programs and would be open to future federal legislation as adoption expands, particularly for universal pre-K and tuition-free public higher education.

For postsecondary education, he supports tuition-free public colleges as well as tuition-free public trade, technical and vocational schools. Private colleges and universities could remain independent and continue charging tuition.

Boris supports maintaining academic competition within a tuition-free public system. College admissions could continue to consider grades, academic performance and other appropriate measures; making public higher education free would not mean eliminating academic standards or selective admissions.

He also supports full forgiveness of outstanding federal student-loan debt. His proposal does not presently call for federal forgiveness of privately issued student loans.

Beyond tuition, Yagudayev supports exploring comprehensive assistance for students who need help with required educational and living costs, potentially including books, food, transportation and housing. He is considering linking full support for these additional needs to a community or public-service component, but the structure and requirements of such a program remain under development.

The federal-state funding structure, eligibility and provider model for universal child care, treatment of non-tuition college expenses, admissions rules across different public institutions and any service component attached to comprehensive student support remain to be developed.

ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE

Public participation in AI-era wealth

“The massive gains and changes we will see from AI need to be broadly shared by all Americans.”

Boris supports a proactive governmental role in the development and regulation of artificial intelligence. He favors exploring public-private ownership and investment structures that could give the public a financial stake in technologies capable of substantially changing employment and productivity.

Over the medium and long term, he supports creating a national wealth fund connected to economic gains generated by AI. One mechanism he proposes exploring is a value-added tax on AI-related products and services, with public revenues directed toward such a fund.

Internationally, Yagudayev supports pursuing a rules-based framework for artificial intelligence that would establish processes for regulation, verification and international cooperation, including rules addressing military uses of AI, AI-enabled crime and economic disruption.

The tax base and rate, fund governance, public-private ownership model and international verification structure remain proposals for further development.

DEMOCRACY & CAMPAIGN FINANCE

Participation, access & public financing

“Democracy is democracy only if it’s open to all, not just the wealthy and well-connected.”

Boris supports public financing and public matching for federal campaigns, ranked-choice voting and reforms intended to expand access to the electoral process for voters and prospective candidates.

He supports overturning the constitutional rule established by Citizens United v. FEC, including through constitutionally permissible reforms. The Supreme Court's 2010 decision invalidated federal prohibitions on corporate independent expenditures and electioneering communications while leaving disclosure requirements and the ban on direct corporate contributions in place.

Existing federal public campaign financing is centered on presidential elections. Boris's position would extend a public-financing or matching framework to federal congressional campaigns and therefore would require new federal legislation.

His broader stated principle is greater direct voter participation and more equal access to political candidacy.

Legal context: See the Federal Election Commission's materials on Citizens United v. FEC and public funding of presidential elections.

DEMOCRACY & BALLOT ACCESS

Constituent Assemblies & a Cashless Path to the Ballot

“Petitioning should be more than collecting a signature. It can be an opportunity for residents to help build the platform a candidate is asking them to put on the ballot.”

At the root of Boris's proposal is a ballot-access problem: prospective candidates who lack substantial campaign funding, established political connections or the advantages of incumbency may have fewer organizational resources available to collect the signatures required to reach the ballot. He proposes the constituent-assembly model as an efficient, volunteer-centered way for such candidates to organize a serious ballot-access effort while giving residents a substantial role in shaping the policy agenda attached to the candidacy.

The model is designed around a single candidate and combines ballot-access petitioning with meaningful resident participation in developing that candidate's policy agenda. During the legally permitted petitioning period, a campaign using the model would organize public constituent assemblies in towns and communities throughout the district.

Rather than treating petitioning primarily as a brief request for a signature, each gathering would function as both a civic policy workshop and a petition-gathering event. Residents could divide into groups addressing priorities such as local issues, healthcare, taxes, housing, labor, foreign policy and other subjects selected by participants. Each group would develop proposals and bring them before the full local assembly for discussion and a recorded vote.

Artificial intelligence could serve as an administrative tool to transcribe proposals, identify substantially similar ideas, organize competing versions and prepare draft summaries. AI would not determine policy or cast votes. Residents would review the language and decide which proposals their local assembly adopts.

The approved proposals from assemblies across the district would then be aggregated into a district-wide set of constituent policy goals. The resulting record would show areas of agreement, disagreement and local variation while giving the candidate a platform shaped through direct resident participation.

At the conclusion of an assembly, eligible voters who support placing that candidate on the ballot could voluntarily sign the candidate's official petition in person, subject to election-law requirements. Signing would not be required to participate in the policy process, and participating in the assembly would not obligate anyone to support the candidate in the election.

The aggregated policy record would continue to have a purpose after the campaign. If elected, the candidate could be measured publicly against the priorities residents developed through the assemblies—creating a documented connection between what constituents asked for during ballot access and what the officeholder pursued after taking office.

The model is intended to enrich a petitioning process in which residents may otherwise encounter a petition with little opportunity to discuss the candidate or influence the agenda behind the candidacy. It substitutes organized participation, volunteer labor and repeated local meetings for some of the advantages that campaigns can obtain through paid petition circulators, fundraising networks or established party infrastructure.

As an illustration, forty assemblies averaging fifty attendees would involve roughly 2,000 participants. That would not mean 2,000 legally valid petition signatures: eligibility, party enrollment, duplicate signatures, witness requirements and other election-law rules can reduce the valid total. Organizers would therefore need a compliance process and a margin above the legal minimum.

The proposed model is a voluntary campaign-organizing framework for a candidate already seeking ballot access, not a system for selecting among competing candidates and not a replacement for state election law. Meeting procedures, voting rules, AI transparency standards, accessibility requirements and methods for aggregating district-wide policy results remain to be developed.

New York context: For the 2026 primary cycle, New York required a congressional designating petition to contain signatures from 5% of active enrolled voters of the relevant political party or 1,250 voters, whichever was less. Electronic petition signatures were not accepted, and party designating petitions were subject to signer and witness requirements. Federal election rules generally permit individuals to provide uncompensated personal volunteer services to campaigns, while expenditures and paid activity can be subject to separate campaign-finance rules.

PROPOSAL IN DEVELOPMENT

The “Citizens App”

“Technology can and should bring more people into democracy.”

Boris proposes exploring a publicly funded civic social-media platform, which he calls the Citizens App. The proposed platform would organize civic information according to a verified user's jurisdiction, allowing people to identify government offices and officeholders from the local through federal levels.

Under the concept, users could choose to examine pending and proposed legislation and participate in nonbinding public polling on those measures. Boris envisions the resulting polling as an additional source of constituent feedback for elected officials.

The proposal also contemplates candidate profiles, candidate registration functions and electronic support for ballot-access petitions where applicable law permits them. Boris proposes that jurisdictions be able to opt into the system rather than having the platform automatically replace existing state and local election procedures.

Important implementation questions—including identity verification, privacy, cybersecurity, separation of verified identity from political-expression data, election-law compliance and administration—remain to be developed.

FOREIGN POLICY & PEACE

Peace as the first objective

“Peace is practical. Peace is necessary.”

Boris argues that because U.S. military, diplomatic and economic decisions have global consequences, American foreign policy should place preventing and ending armed conflict at the center of its objectives. He summarizes this principle as: “Peace needs to be a first goal, not an end goal.”

He supports limiting U.S. military intervention primarily to defensive and narrowly defined tactical operations rather than prolonged or large-scale offensive wars. Where military action is considered unavoidable, he favors limited operations, including special operations where appropriate, over total-war mobilization.

He supports strategically reducing defense spending while maintaining military capacity for national defense and limited operations. He also opposes U.S. taxpayer-funded military support to governments or forces determined to be committing war crimes. Existing U.S. law already restricts certain assistance to foreign security-force units when there is credible information of gross violations of human rights; Yagudayev's stated position is broader in scope.

Diplomatically, Yagudayev favors negotiated settlements that link security arrangements with political reforms. He views international diplomacy as requiring reciprocal concessions and argues that long-term peace should be pursued alongside greater democratic participation and economic cooperation.

His approach also emphasizes maintaining the United States' ability to expand industrial and military production rapidly if a major war nevertheless becomes unavoidable.

The precise scale of proposed defense reductions, authorization standards for tactical interventions and legal mechanism for restricting assistance remain to be developed.

Legal context: U.S. Leahy laws restrict certain assistance to foreign security-force units where the government has credible information implicating a unit in gross violations of human rights.

RUSSIA & UKRAINE

A negotiated settlement tied to democratic reform

“The future of democracy is worth an exchange of land.”

Boris supports diplomacy aimed at ending the Russia-Ukraine war through a negotiated settlement. His proposal would permit Russia to retain Ukrainian territory it currently controls, including Crimea, in exchange for verifiable democratic reforms and greater democratic political participation inside Russia.

He argues that democratization in Russia could reduce the long-term potential for future conflict and describes that objective as the rationale for linking territorial concessions to political reform.

Boris also points to his Russian ethnic background as part of his personal perspective on engaging Russian society and making the case for such a settlement.

This is Yagudayev's proposed negotiated settlement; the description does not characterize Russian sovereignty over occupied Ukrainian territory as internationally recognized.

ISRAEL & PALESTINE

International separation & two states

“Only when friends hold friends accountable is peace possible.”

Boris supports international intervention by a coalition of allies in Gaza and the West Bank intended to separate armed parties and protect Palestinian and Israeli civilians during a transition.

He supports establishing a Palestinian state alongside Israel on the basis of the pre-1967 lines, with security arrangements intended to protect both populations.

The pre-1967 territorial basis is also reflected in the United Nations' current two-state framework, although the specific security and political mechanisms in Yagudayev's proposal are his own.

The composition, mandate, authorization, duration and rules of engagement of the proposed international presence remain to be developed.

International context: United Nations statements and the 2025 New York Declaration describe a two-state solution based on the pre-1967/1967 lines and include discussion of international support and security guarantees.

CHINA & TAIWAN

Security, reciprocity & political opening

“Internationally, we should learn from one another if we are to grow as a global community.”

Boris supports diplomacy with China built around reciprocal political, economic and security concessions. His stated approach would connect long-term security arrangements concerning Taiwan with a verifiable opening toward greater democratic participation within China.

He argues more broadly that peaceful competition between the United States and China should include willingness by each country to learn from aspects of the other's system rather than treating the relationship exclusively as a zero-sum confrontation.

He summarizes that philosophy by saying that, in his view, China would benefit from greater democracy while the United States would benefit from adopting more social-democratic or socialistic economic policies.

The specific diplomatic conditions, verification mechanisms and security guarantees associated with this proposal remain to be developed.

OTHER DOCUMENTED POSITIONS

Cannabis policy

Boris has publicly supported cannabis legalization and liberalization of federal cannabis policy.

Drug policy & social assistance

He has advocated addressing drug-related social problems with greater emphasis on assistance, inclusion and underlying social conditions rather than primarily through enforcement.

Political identity

Boris has publicly used Democratic and communist terminology to describe his political identity and has also associated his politics with democratic socialism.

Political communication

He has described social media as a modern public square and uses online platforms as a principal venue for his political communication.
